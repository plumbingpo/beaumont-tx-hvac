How Dallas Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

The best way to avoid a mid-season breakdown is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners replace filters every one to three months, keep shrubs and grass trimmed two feet back from the outdoor condenser, rinse the condenser coil each spring, and have a professional tune-up twice a year, cooling in spring and heating in fall, so small problems get caught while they're still inexpensive.

How old is your system? Its age says a lot about what comes next. With regular service, most air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is past that point and also shows rising bills, uneven temperatures, or repeated repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install in peak season.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
